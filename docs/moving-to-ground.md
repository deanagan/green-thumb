To position these two roses successfully next to each other, we need to look at both the natural sun trajectory in Sydney and the unique growth habits of these two specific David Austin varieties.
In the Southern Hemisphere, the winter and early spring sun tracks lower across the **northern** sky. This means any obstruction (like a fence, wall, or even a larger neighboring plant) to the north, east, or west will cast shadows, creating a distinct "less sun" side on the **south** or **south-west** of a planting run.
Here is how to strategically place, distance, and align 'Lady of Shalott' and 'The Mill on the Floss' next week:
## 1. Which Rose Should Get the Position with Less Sun?
You should place **'Lady of Shalott' on the side that gets less sun**, and give **'The Mill on the Floss' the sunnier spot.**
 * **Why 'Lady of Shalott' handles less sun:** David Austin’s orange and apricot roses are incredibly resilient. 'Lady of Shalott' is widely recognized as one of their most shade-tolerant varieties. It will still bloom reliably, maintain its rich color without bleaching, and resist disease exceptionally well even with only 4 to 5 hours of direct sun.
 * **Why 'The Mill on the Floss' needs more sun:** This variety features deeply cupped, delicate pink blossoms with a distinct dark pink picotee edge. To crisp up that beautiful edge and prevent the blooms from "balling" (failing to open in cool, damp weather), it requires as much strong, direct sunlight as possible. Additionally, pink varieties are generally more prone to black spot if relegated to partial shade.
## 2. How Far Apart Should You Distance Them?
Because you are training both of these vigorous roses against a system of overlapping U-shape trainers, you should space the main planting centers **1.5 to 1.8 meters apart**.
 * **Why this distance?** Both of these roses naturally want to grow into massive shrubs or short climbers (reaching 1.8 to 2.5 meters in height and spread).
 * If you plant them closer than 1.5 meters, their vigorous growth will quickly collide in the middle. This ruins the clean, minimalist look of your U-trainer architecture, creates a tangled mess of thorns, and chokes out air circulation—increasing the risk of fungal issues on the shaded side.
 * Spacing them around 1.5 to 1.8 meters gives you enough room to gracefully fan out the canes of 'Lady of Shalott' to the left and 'The Mill on the Floss' to the right without them completely swallowing each other.
## Next Week's Quick Setup Checklist:
 1. **Plot the Run:** Mark out your two planting spots, ensuring they are at least 1.5 meters apart from center to center.
 2. **Assign the Spots:** Place 'The Mill on the Floss' on the left or right depending on which side receives the most uninhibited northern/eastern sunlight. Place 'Lady of Shalott' on the side facing the shadow or obstruction.
 3. **Mound Up:** Because you are moving them into the ground, build your soil upward into gentle mounds rather than digging deep pockets, ensuring excellent drainage for both.
 4. **Install the Trainers:** Anchor your overlapping U-trainers firmly behind the mounds before planting, so you can immediately tie down the bare canes without disturbing the new root zones.
